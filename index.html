Her calendar

She keeps her own calendarపండుగలు

Some of her days are marked in lamps, some in chariots — and one comes once in twelve years. Come when she is celebrating.

26 JUN – 7 JUL 2027once in 12 years

Godavari Pushkaralu

When Jupiter enters Leo, a dip in her water is believed to wash twelve years clean. Crores of pilgrims will fill the ghats from Rajahmundry to Kovvuru. The next time she holds the festival is 2027. Twelve days. Plan early.

MID-JANUARYKanuma day

Prabhala Theertham

Shiva rides out of eleven Konaseema villages on towering prabhalu, carried on shoulders through canals and paddy to a coconut grove at Jaggannatota. Four centuries running.

LATE JAN – FEBMagha month

Antarvedi Kalyanam

Where she meets the sea, Narasimha weds Lakshmi, and the next day a wooden chariot the size of a house is pulled through town by lakhs of hands.

JUL – SEPin flood

Pulasa season

The river runs red, the hilsa swims up from the sea and becomes pulasa, Andhra’s costliest fish. The saying goes it’s worth pawning gold for one.

AUG – SEPten days

Ganesh Nimajjanam

Ten days of pandals end at her edge: drums, dancing, and Ganesha lowered into the swollen river at Pushkar Ghat.

NOVEMBERthe lamp month

Karthika Masam

A month of pre-dawn dips in the city they call Dakshina Kashi, and at dusk, rafts of oil lamps set adrift, hundreds of flames on the dark water.

Fly into Rajahmundry (RJA) or rail to Rajamahendravaram Junction — cross the river on the road-rail bridge for the best first look.

October–February is river weather. July–September the monsoon makes her loud and the fields their greenest.

03

A day in the nursery capital

Walk two or three working Kadiyam nurseries with the families who run them — grafting and bonsai up close, then the flower market at full colour.

Uma Markandeyeswara and Kotilingeswara on the ghats, Korukonda’s 615-step hilltop shrine, and the thousand-year Golingeshwara cluster at Biccavolu — one day, one river of faith.

The 1852 anicut that turned the delta into a rice bowl — and the museum that tells the story of the engineer who built it.

Getting here

By air

Rajahmundry Airport (RJA), 18 km out at Madhurapudi. Direct flights include Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Chennai.

By rail

Rajamahendravaram Junction, on the Howrah–Chennai main line — the train crosses the Godavari on the rail bridges as it arrives.

By road

On NH16: about 160 km from Vijayawada, 200 from Visakhapatnam. Dindi is 80 km south through the delta’s coconut lanes.

By boat

Papikondalu launches board upriver at the Gandi Pochamma Temple point — operators pick you up in Rajahmundry in the morning.

A DAY ON
THE GHATS

The river’s whole day, lived at her edge.

scroll into the day

05:40

The river wakes

Walk down before the town does. At first light the ghats belong to pilgrims, brass pots and mist — and the Godavari, wide as a sea, barely moving.

08:00

The temples at her edge

Uma Markandeyeswara. Kotilingeswara. Shrines the ghats have held for centuries, each a few steps from the water that made them.

12:30

An Andhra noon

Rice on a banana leaf. Gongura, avakaya, ghee. Eaten the way this district has always eaten — generously, and without hurry.

17:30

The river turns gold

Back to the steps as the light lowers. A slow country boat — or a speedboat, if you like your gold with spray.

19:00

Harathi

Fire returned to the water. Bells, flame, and the whole ghat singing to the river that raised it. This is what you came for.
