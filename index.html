01Welcome to East Godavari

Andhra Pradesh’s cultural capital

Rajamahendravaram, still Rajahmundry to most, sits where the Godavari runs wide as a sea. It is the city of Nannaya, who began the Telugu Mahabharatam here a thousand years ago, and of the evening harathi on its ghats. In 2027 the Pushkaralu bring millions here to bathe.

  • 01

    By air

    Rajahmundry Airport (RJA) is at Madhurapudi, about 15 km from the city. IndiGo flies direct to Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi and Mumbai; Fly91 to Hyderabad and Tirupati; Alliance Air to Tirupati. Schedules change, so check with the airline.

  • 02

    By rail

    Rajahmundry station (RJY) is on the Howrah–Chennai main line, and the city has a second station, Godavari (GVN), right by the ghats. Coming from the Vijayawada side, the train crosses the Godavari on the long bridges into the city.

  • 03

    By road

    Rajamahendravaram sits on NH16, the Chennai–Kolkata highway, with direct roads to Vijayawada and Visakhapatnam and buses from across Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.

  • 04

    On the river

    Papikondalu cruises leave from the Gandi Pochamma boat point, upriver in Polavaram district; APTDC’s day tour picks up in Rajamahendravaram at 7 am and is back by about 8 pm. Boats pause whenever the river runs high, usually from July, so check before you plan.

01Boats and the river

Papikondalu boats run only when the Godavari is below its safety level at the Polavaram project, so services pause in high water, often from July into the autumn. Everyone on board wears a life jacket. Check with APTDC or your operator the day before.

02Visiting temples

Cover shoulders and knees, leave footwear at the gate, and keep phones away inside the sanctum. Festival days bring long queues: go early.

03Language

Telugu is spoken everywhere, in the soft Godavari accent. English and Hindi work in hotels and on the boats, and a “namaskaram” opens most doors.

04Money

UPI works almost everywhere, small eateries included. Carry some cash for ferries, temple counters and roadside coconut, and use ATMs in the towns.

05Food

Pack Tapeswaram’s kaja and Atreyapuram’s pootharekulu for the road, and in the monsoon ask for pulasa.

06If something goes wrong

Dial 112 for police, fire or ambulance (108 also reaches an ambulance). The big hospitals are in Rajamahendravaram and Kakinada. On Hope Island, don’t count on shade or food until your boat returns.

the ghats

The river’s whole day, lived at its edge.

Scroll into the day

First light

Down to the river

Walk down before the town does. At first light the ghats belong to pilgrims, brass pots and mist, and the Godavari, wide as a sea, barely moving.

Morning

The temples at the water’s edge

Uma Markandeyeswara. Kotilingeswara. Shrines the ghats have held for centuries, each a few steps from the water that made them.

Noon

An Andhra noon

Rice on a banana leaf. Gongura, avakaya, ghee. Eaten the way this district has always eaten, generously, and without hurry.

Sunset

The river turns gold

Back to the steps as the light lowers. A slow country boat, or a speedboat, if you like your gold with spray.

After dark

Harathi

Fire returned to the water. Bells, flame, and the whole ghat singing to the river.

Early morning

Boarding upriver

Set out early from Rajamahendravaram for the Gandi Pochamma boat point. The launch fills slowly, with families and chai, and breakfast is served on deck.

The river widens

The Godavari opens until both banks go quiet. Fishermen. Sadhus. Herons. Nothing needs your attention, and that is the point.

Late morning

The hills close in

The banks begin to rise, and the river that seemed endless starts to narrow into green.

Papikondalu

The hills close behind the boat like a door. After that, the only sound is water.
